Where the product is

The current app (106 files, about 48,900 lines, built in three days of AI-agent commits) does many things shallowly. Its core promise does not work by default: no transcript is produced unless the user finds and enables live transcription, and then only the first utterance is captured. Several scores measure something other than their label. Of 90 audited features, 12 are implemented as documented, 41 are partial, 16 are broken and 10 are duplicated. Privacy text contradicts the code, speaking-session saves are rejected by the database rules, account deletion is unreachable, and CI can publish debug-signed builds without running a single test.

What is recommended Proposed

One core loop: a ten-minute daily session → speak → see the transcript → three labelled metrics (Measured or Estimated) → one strength → the mistakes the checks found, with evidence → coach tips → try again. When evidence is insufficient the app says Inconclusive instead of guessing. Feedback comes from deterministic logic and on-device analysis; no cloud AI is needed. Four tabs (Today · Practice · Words · Progress), offline operation, and an optional account. The curriculum is built from the techniques taught on the @askvinh channel, re-expressed in original wording with a link to each source video, under the Product Owner's decision D-08 Decided 2026-11-21.

How we get there

  • R0 Foundation — safety, CI gates, removals, data foundation and the speech spike: about 38–55 days †.
  • R1 Core loop MVP — about 107–156 engineering days †, plus about 38 person-days † of content verification, mostly by the Product Owner, in parallel.
  • R2 Depth — role-play, check-ins, notebook and quiz, inbox, more content: about 31–46 days †.
  • R3 Reach — store launch, video self-review and recognition research, each only after its own decision.

What the Product Owner needs to do

Review this book and the package behind it, record decisions D-01…D-36 (D-08 is already decided), approve gates G1–G4, and give the explicit instruction "start Stage B". The biggest open risks are on-device speech recognition quality on real devices (RSK-01, decided by spike SPK-01), single-developer capacity (RSK-06) and the content-verification effort (RSK-04, RSK-17).

Product definition

Where SpeakCraft starts from, who it is for, what it will and will not do, what each capability contains, and how the work is prioritised.

Chapter 1Where we start

SpeakCraft today is a demo of many features, not a product. It has the surface area of a mature platform but no single experience that reliably delivers its promise. The root problem is not visual design or missing features; it is the absence of product focus, combined with a development process that added features and certified them without verification.

RD-07 §4; RD-09 §2

1.5 Root causes of dissatisfaction

The final review condenses the audit into five root causes:

  1. No product definition. Features accumulated per request without a target user, a core loop or non-goals, so effort spread across roughly fifteen products in one app.
  2. Claims ahead of evidence. Features were declared "verified" without tests, and scores were produced where the app had no evidence, so users had no reason to trust the numbers.
  3. Oscillating AI strategy. Cloud Gemini → "zero cloud AI" → "hybrid AI coach" in three days left dormant code paths and contradictory privacy text.
  4. No engineering foundation. No CI tests, no single source of truth for data, manual dependency injection created inside a composable, and duplicated engines, so every change risked regressions.
  5. Borrowed identity without a basis. The product's name, tab and content leaned on a third-party creator with no permission or verifiable sources. Decision D-08 now resolves this: the techniques are kept and the branding is dropped.

What the audit recommends
  1. Stop adding features. Re-found the product on one segment and one loop.

SpeakCraft does not need more features. It needs one excellent, trustworthy core experience and the discipline to say no to everything that does not serve it. RD-13 §1; PLN-05 §3

2.1 Vision and mission Proposed

Vision Everyone who can speak English should be able to speak it with confidence — at work, in interviews and in everyday life.
Mission SpeakCraft gives intermediate English speakers a private, honest speaking coach in their pocket: ten minutes of real speaking practice a day, feedback grounded in evidence they can see, and progress they can measure — processed on their own phone.

RD-13 §2–3

The primary problem

People with good English knowledge still freeze, ramble or sound unsure when they must speak, because they rarely practise speaking aloud, never hear themselves objectively, and have no safe, private way to rehearse the situations that matter to them. Existing apps either teach vocabulary and grammar rather than speaking, require an internet connection and send the user's voice to cloud services, or give scores users do not trust.

RD-13 §6

Differentiation Proposed

  1. Honesty as a feature. No competitor shows measurement confidence or refuses to score bad audio. SpeakCraft shows what was measured, how sure it is and what it could not measure on every result.
  2. Private and offline-capable. Core practice and evaluation run on the device; sign-in is optional; recordings never leave the phone by default.
  3. Speaking for work, for non-native speakers. Interview, meeting and presentation practice designed around the needs of B1–B2 speakers.
  4. Small, focused, calm. Ten minutes a day, four tabs, no manipulative streak pressure.

SpeakCraft deliberately does not compete on phoneme-level diagnosis, open-ended AI conversation, avatars, exam-score prediction, human tutors or content volume.

RD-15 §3

Learning philosophy

  1. Output beats input. Every session includes real speaking attempts.
  2. One focus at a time. Each result shows the mistakes the checks actually found (a short, evidenced list — at most three shown first) and coach tips, led by one strength and one thing to change next.
  3. Rehearse the real situation — the interview answer, the meeting update, the self-introduction — then transfer.
  4. Repetition with variation. Retry the same prompt; revisit skills with new prompts; review words at spaced intervals.
  5. Listen to yourself. Seeing the transcript and hearing the recording is itself feedback.
  6. Measure honestly, celebrate effort fairly. Streaks for genuine practice; ability claims only from evidence.

14.2 How stories are written

  • Each story follows the form "As <persona or user class>, I want <capability>, so that <benefit>", with three to five Given/When/Then acceptance criteria and two to four edge cases pointing at the governing rule.
  • Personas are named (Aarav, Meera, Daniel, Sofia, Hoang). User classes are Local user, Signed-in user and Beta tester.
  • Priority and release are inherited from the traced product requirement; a story tracing several requirements takes the highest priority.
  • Where a decision is pending, acceptance criteria follow the consolidated recommendation and say so. D-08 is applied as binding.
  • Acceptance criteria that go beyond the SRS are marked RECOMMENDATION and become binding only on approval.

Appendix BGlossary

TermMeaning
ActivityA content item — prompt, template and metadata — from the bundled content pack.
AttemptOne recording of the user responding to an activity or word stage, plus its evaluation.
Valid attemptAn attempt that passed the evidence gate. Only valid attempts count toward completion, streaks and practice time.
Capability (CAP-nn)One of the twelve product capabilities defined in RD-16, each with one feature specification.
Coach tipsAfter a valid attempt, the focus "Try this next" plus at most two further tips, selected deterministically from technique tips in the content pack and linked to the technique. Word results get one tip.
Content packThe bundled, versioned content shipped with the app: activities, techniques, tips and words, each with provenance.
Content freezeThe point in R1 after which the content shipped in the release no longer changes; content verification must be finished by then.
dateKeyThe local calendar date (YYYY-MM-DD) in the device time zone at the time of an event. Streaks and daily plans use it.
D-08The Product Owner's decision of 2026-11-21, "original wording, link out", for creator-derived content: own wording and examples, no verbatim transcripts, a link to the specific source video, no creator name, likeness or branding.
EstimatedA metric label: a heuristic inference (for example filler rate, lexical variety, grammar observations, structure elements). Estimated issues read as "Possible …".
EventAn immutable learning fact with a UUID that makes writes and sync idempotent.
Evidence gateThe check that admits an attempt only if it has enough recording, voiced speech and input level (and a transcript where the template needs one).
Gate (G1–G7)A Product Owner approval point. G1–G4 in Stage A; G5 per epic, G6 and G7 per release in Stage B.
Golden testA test that runs fixed inputs through evaluation and compares the output with a stored expected result, so that the same attempt always gets the same feedback.
InconclusiveThe outcome when evidence is insufficient: not a pass, not a fail. The reason is shown with a retry; it never counts as completion, failure, a streak day or practice time.
Issue code (ISSUE_*)A detectable problem in an attempt (for example ISSUE_FILLERS_HIGH, ISSUE_PACE_FAST), raised from metrics and qualified rules.
Local profileThe on-device user identity used before or without sign-in.
MeasuredA metric label: computed directly from audio or transcript (for example duration, pace, long pauses, word count, recognised-as-intended %).
MetricA quantity computed from an attempt, always labelled Measured or Estimated; null and shown as "Not available" when its inputs are missing.
Mistakes foundThe result section listing the issues the checks detected in an attempt, each with its evidence and label; first three shown, "Show all" for the rest. When nothing is found it says so, without claiming the attempt was perfect.
Noise bandThe range within which a change in a metric is treated as "no clear change"; issues and trends are reported only beyond it.
Not runThe status of every specified test case in Stage A: written but never executed.
Provenance / licence statusThe record of where a content item comes from (source video, timestamp, reviewer, date) and its status, for example ORIGINAL_REEXPRESSION or PENDING_REVIEW. Unverified items are excluded from release builds.
Recognised as intendedThe share of target words the recogniser returned as expected. It is never called pronunciation or accent accuracy.
Release (R0–R3)R0 Foundation · R1 Core loop MVP · R2 Depth · R3 Reach (conditional).
Self-checkA short "Check yourself" list for aspects audio cannot show (for example eye contact). Ticks are labelled Self-reported and never affect metrics or progress.
SessionToday's ordered set of steps (activities and word tasks) for a local date.
StepOne unit of a session: a speaking activity, a word stage or a review set.
Spike (SPK-01/02/03)A time-boxed investigation. SPK-01: on-device recognition on real devices; SPK-02: grammar-rule precision corpus; SPK-03: Whisper-class recognition research (R3).
Stage A / Stage BStage A is discovery and planning (documentation only). Stage B is implementation, which starts only after G4 and the instruction "start Stage B".
Streak dayA local date with at least one valid speaking attempt from any source. Session completion is not required.
TechniqueA first-class content item: a communication framework or technique taught on the source channel, re-expressed in our own words with its idea, steps, exercises, tips and a link to the specific source video.
Techniques collectionThe list of techniques in the Practice tab, opening the technique detail screen SCR-PRAC-04.
TemplateThe interaction type: Read aloud, Listen & repeat, Answer a question, Structured answer, Tell a story (R1); Scripted role-play (R2).
Tier (T1, T2, T3a, T3b)The device's speech capability: T1 continuous on-device transcription with replay; T2 on-device transcription without replay; T3a online transcription with consent; T3b audio-only feedback.
TrackOne of five practice areas: Everyday conversation, Workplace, Interviews, Presentations & storytelling, Clear speech.
"Watch the original lesson"The link on a technique that opens its specific source video, at its timestamp, outside the app. It never names the creator.
Word stagesLearn → Say it → Use it → Make your own → Review, passed in order, with pass thresholds for stages 2–4.
